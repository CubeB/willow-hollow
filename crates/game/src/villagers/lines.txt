# What the people of the settlements say when greeted (#204).
#
# Each [section] is a list of lines, one to a line, and one is picked by the person and
# how often they have met you. A greeting is an opening and then a remark. Slots are
# filled from the place they live:
#   {town}       their own settlement
#   {neighbour}  the nearest other settlement
#   {river}      the river or lake nearest them
#   {season}     spring, summer, autumn or winter
#   {time}       morning, afternoon or evening
#   {trade}      their work, in a word
# A line with a slot the place has nothing for (a town on no river) is never picked.

[open first]
Good {time} to you. You're not from {town}, are you?
Well met, traveller. Welcome to {town}.
Good {time}. Don't often see a new face in {town}.
Oh, hello. Mind the lane, it's a bit uneven. Welcome to {town}.
A good {time} to you, stranger. {town} is glad of the company.

[open again]
Back again? Good {time} to you.
Good {time}! I hoped we'd see you in {town} again.
Ah, it's you. Good {time}.
Hello again. You've found your way round {town} by now, I expect.

[open often]
Good {time}, friend. You're nearly one of us now.
There you are! {town} wouldn't be the same without you wandering about.
Good {time}. Sit a while, if you've the time.

[open child]
Hello! Who are you?
Good {time}! Are you a traveller? Have you been to {neighbour}?
Hello! I'm not supposed to talk to strangers. But you seem nice.

[remark river]
The {river} has been kind to us this {season}.
If you're walking on, follow the {river}. It's the gentlest way.
Fish are rising in the {river} this {season}, they say.
Folk say the {river} was here before {town} was, and it'll be here after.

[remark neighbour]
If you're headed to {neighbour}, it's not far. Give them my regards.
My cousin lives over in {neighbour}. Fine folk, if a little proud.
They make good bread in {neighbour}, but don't tell anyone here I said so.
You'll find a bed in {neighbour} if you walk on before dark.

[remark tree]
Have you seen the village tree on the green? It's older than any of us.
We meet under the village tree when there's news. Not often, mind.
My grandmother planted acorns by the village tree. One of them took.

[remark season]
{season} suits {town}, I always think.
It's a fair {season} in {town} this year.
We'll have a long {season} of it yet, in {town}.
Nothing like {season} in {town}. You picked a good time to come.

[remark trade]
Work as a {trade} keeps me busy in {town}, but there's always time for a word.
Plenty to do for a {trade} in {town} this {season}.
Mind how you go. And if you need a {trade} in {town}, you know where I am.

[remark child]
I'm going to the green to play. All the children of {town} play there.
Have you seen the village tree? I can nearly reach the lowest branch.
When I'm grown I'm going to walk all the way to {neighbour}.

[remark elder]
I've seen a good many {season}s in {town}. This one's a kind one.
When I was young, {town} was half the size. Now look at it.
The {river} froze right over one winter, when I was small. You could walk to the far bank.
Sit with me a moment. My knees don't walk to {neighbour} any more.
